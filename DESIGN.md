# Interview Practice design

The study screen answers one question: what decision would you make with the system’s current constraints? The current prompt is the primary task. The architecture explains the same stage, and grows only when its answer is revealed.

Use a flat black ground, #111 surfaces, #2a2a2a nested controls, #fff primary text, #b4b4b4 secondary text, and #949494 metadata. Use IBM Plex Sans at 400, 500, and 600. Mint (#b9e7cf) marks focus, the selected rating, and changed architecture components. Official service icons identify AWS products. Primary actions use white with black text. Keep controls at least 44px tall, panels unshadowed, and body text readable.

The workspace uses the full browser width and available height. A compact text header holds the scenario title and session controls without a logo or separate masthead. Desktop pairs a readable question column with a diagram that fills the remaining space. Tablet and phone stack them. Narrow diagrams put each labeled outgoing connection under its source component. Expanded diagrams have button-operated zoom and component descriptions. Notes are optional. Native dialogs retain keyboard focus and support Escape. Reduced motion disables transitions.

Only revealed questions belong in the session path. Render diagrams from the active question or answer frame without reserving positions, labels, edges, or thumbnails for future changes. Authored desktop positions stay stable as the design grows. Larger inline diagrams focus on the relevant established subsystem and show the count of established components. The expanded view includes the full current frame. A reference design is one valid approach in a hypothetical exercise.

History preserves completed paths, notes, and self-ratings across new sessions. Its dialog shows only answered questions. Opening a reference answer reveals its rubric, alternatives, mistakes, sources, and a button for its saved diagram. That diagram uses the question's original content revision, with zoom and a text equivalent. Escape returns focus to the same history item. Closed answers and diagrams unmount their contents; closing history removes its records so practice does not preview previous branches. Adding follow-ups cannot reopen an earlier completion. Earlier local saves and backups migrate without resetting their question or reveal phase. Pending sync requests retain their original payload for idempotent retries.

Food delivery, chat, URL shortening, news feeds, and file sync each have two starting variants with nine authored questions per variant. A native, labeled family selector uses a 44px target. Switching families saves the unfinished session, including its question, notes, rating, reveal phase, and optional 20-minute deadline. New sessions avoid the latest starting variant in the chosen family. Continuing chooses a valid child weighted toward due, weak, and unseen questions and avoids the previous continuation when alternatives exist. Each child continues its parent’s reference design. Each question has its own FSRS schedule.

The one-row mode bar holds Scenarios, Review, and Library. The more button opens account, private cards, and backup actions. The daily overview shows counts before and after a review. During a review, the prompt is the heading and the progress counter stays compact. The answer and rubric appear on reveal, with alternatives, pitfalls, and sources in a disclosure. Ratings show their intervals and remain editable until continuing. A review fixes its queue when it begins and puts due questions first. The default is five new items per study day and FSRS-6 targets 90% recall. Rated scenario questions can enter daily review and use the same full-frame diagram dialog.

The library puts actual counts beside the daily review, coding, and recall actions. A design panel lists families without showing their follow-ups. Search and topic filters narrow the 72 recall cards and 42 coding exercises. Drills reuse the focused recall view and preserve notes and position. Daily review handles scheduling. The coding checklist links to the original problem and reveals a separate reasoning check on demand. Completed checkboxes mark self-reported practice.

Private editing uses labeled 48px fields and preserved local drafts. Deleting a card asks for confirmation and removes its associated study data. Native backup dialogs preview an import before replacing the workspace and offer undo during that page visit. Account loading has a plain status. Failed sync keeps the exact local draft and retry request. Concurrent edits show both choices explicitly, with a download action before choosing. Guest and account caches remain separate, including on sign-out.
